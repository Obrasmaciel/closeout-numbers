The Punch List Checklist

What to walk, in what order, and how to stop the list from reopening.

Free. No email required. Built for the contractor closing out a residential remodel.

The two rules that decide how this goes

1. You walk it before the client does. Every item you find is a repair. Every item the client finds first is evidence that you don't check your own work. Same defect, completely different conversation.

2. The list closes once. A punch list that accepts new items after it is signed is not a punch list, it is an open account. Say at the walkthrough: this is the list; anything found after today is a warranty item or a new order of work. Then honour warranty properly — that is what makes the boundary fair.

Order of operations

Walk it in this sequence and you will find more with less back-and-forth:

1. Alone, with a light. Bright, raking light along every wall. You will find drywall and paint defects the eye skips under normal light.

2. With your lead, so the person who has to fix it sees it and estimates it.

3. Fix everything found in steps 1 and 2.

4. Then with the client. Not before.

Bring a roll of low-tack tape and mark each item as you go. Photograph the marked item with a reference in frame. A tagged photo settles the argument about whether something was on the list.

Documents you hand over at closeout

Often forgotten, and they turn a good job into a professional one:

  • Warranty letter with the term stated, and what it excludes
  • Manuals and registration info for every appliance and fixture
  • Paint schedule: brand, colour, sheen, per room — the single most appreciated item on this list
  • Tile and flooring model numbers, plus leftover material handed over
  • Filter sizes for anything with a filter
  • Sub contact list for anything under a sub's warranty
  • Final permit sign-off / inspection card copies
  • Lien waivers, where applicable

The number worth tracking

Count the punch items on your next five jobs and tag each with the trade and the cause.

The distribution will not be even. One trade and one cause will produce most of the list — and that is a conversation with a specific sub, or a change to your own sequence, not a general resolution to be more careful.
