The Missed Line Items Checklist

Residential remodel estimating — the costs that don't show up until they're yours.

Free. No email required. Copy it, print it, paste it into your own spreadsheet.

How to use this

Run this list against a bid you have already priced — not against a blank page. The point is not to add every line. The point is to make you say "wait" out loud about four or five of them. Those are the ones that were going to come out of your margin.

Each item says what it is and when it bites, because a checklist without the consequence is just more paperwork.

1. Before the first day (pre-construction)

#Line itemWhen it bites
1Permit fees and the hours spent pulling themMunicipality charges by valuation; you priced the fee, not the two mornings at the counter
2Plan revisions after plan check commentsReviewer wants a detail; your designer bills hourly; nobody budgeted it
3Engineering letter for a beam or headerDiscovered at demo, needed before inspection, stops the job for a week
4Existing-conditions survey / as-built measureYou bid off the client's sketch and it was wrong by 4 inches
5HOA or historic-district approval timeNot a cost line, a schedule line — carrying costs while you wait
6Utility locate and any private locate feePublic locate is free; private side isn't, and you own the damage if you skip it
7Asbestos / lead survey on pre-1980 workLegally required in most jurisdictions; testing is cheap, discovering it mid-demo is not
8Insurance certificate endorsements the client's building requiresCondo boards ask for extra insured; broker charges per certificate

2. Site access, protection, logistics

#Line itemWhen it bites
9Floor, stair and rail protection for the path to the workEvery occupied remodel; damage claims start here
10Dust containment: zip walls, negative air, filter changesClient with allergies or a toddler will make this your problem
11Parking: permits, meters, tickets, or paying for a spaceUrban jobs; a ticket a day for six weeks is a real number
12Long carry / stair carry for material and debrisThird-floor walk-up doubles labor on drywall and tile without warning
13Elevator reservation and building access feesCondos charge for the padded elevator and the doorman's time
14Temporary power, temporary heat, temporary toiletWinter work stalls without heat; nobody prices the propane
15Storage of client's furniture / owner's material"Just put it in the garage" until the garage is the work area
16Security of tools and material on an occupied siteLockbox, job box, or the cost of replacing what walks off

3. Demolition and the unknowns behind it

#Line itemWhen it bites
17Selective demo labor separate from bulk demoSaving a wall costs more than removing it; bulk pricing hides this
18Dumpster pulls — the second and third oneEveryone prices one dumpster. Nobody prices the swap-outs
19Disposal surcharges: mattresses, appliances, tile, concreteLandfills charge by material class, not just weight
20Hazmat disposal if the survey comes back positiveSeparate hauler, manifest, and certified disposal
21Unforeseen framing repair: rot, termite, prior bad workThe allowance you didn't set becomes the change order they fight
22Bringing existing wiring, venting, or plumbing to current codeInspector triggers it; the client thinks you should have known
23Patching where the old work ends and yours beginsThe "feather-out" line — drywall, flooring, and paint transitions
24Temporary shoring for a load-bearing changePriced with the beam, forgotten as a separate labor day

4. The trades most often under-scoped

#Line itemWhen it bites
25Electrical: AFCI/GFCI upgrades triggered by the workCode requires it in the circuits you touch; not in the fixture count
26Electrical: panel capacity check, and the upgrade if it failsNew induction range or heat pump exceeds the service
27Plumbing: relocating a vent or stack, not just the fixtureMoving a toilet more than a few inches becomes a floor-framing job
28Plumbing: shutoff valves, supply lines, escutcheonsSmall parts, always excluded from the fixture package, always needed
29HVAC: return air sizing when you add or close a roomFails inspection or the room never heats and you get called back
30HVAC: duct sealing / register relocation after ceiling workPriced as "existing to remain" until it isn't
31Framing: blocking for grab bars, TVs, cabinets, curtain rodsCosts almost nothing during framing, costs a wall opening later
32Insulation at exterior walls opened during the workCode upgrade at any wall you expose; easy to miss in the takeoff
33Fire caulking / draft stopping at penetrationsInspector's favorite; cheap material, unpriced labor
34Subfloor prep: leveling, underlayment, deflection fixTile fails without it; discovered after demo

5. Finishes — where allowances leak

#Line itemWhen it bites
35Tile waste factor for pattern, size, and layout10% is not enough for herringbone or a large-format floor
36Trim and transition pieces (bullnose, Schluter, thresholds)Priced per square foot; the edges are per linear foot
37Setting materials by substrate: membrane, thinset, grout, sealerThe tile is the allowance; the system around it is not
38Paint: primer coat on new drywall, plus the ceiling"Two coats" quoted for walls only
39Paint: color changes and accent walls after the factClient picks three colors where you priced one
40Cabinet modifications: fillers, panels, scribing to out-of-square wallsNo wall is square in a remodel; the box price assumes it is
41Countertop template visit, seam decision, cutout chargesFabricator bills each cutout and each visit
42Appliance install labor and the parts kitsEspecially dishwasher, hood, and anything gas
43Hardware: knobs, pulls, hinges, closersSmall dollars, guaranteed to be excluded and needed on the last day
44Interior door slabs: hanging, trimming, hardware boringThe door is cheap, the labor isn't

6. The invisible costs of running the job

#Line itemWhen it bites
45Your own supervision hours, per week, priced at a real rateThe single biggest missed line in owner-operator estimating
46Material pickup runs and delivery feesTwo runs a week for eight weeks, plus your truck and your time
47Restocking fees and return trips for wrong materialHappens on every job; treat it as a percentage, not an accident
48Punch list labor after "completion"Always exists, rarely a line item
49Final clean, plus the pre-final clean nobody countsTwo cleans on most jobs; you priced one
50Callback and warranty reserveIf your callbacks cost 1% of revenue, price 1%
51Client meeting and selection-support timeSelection paralysis is billable time or it is your evening
52Change-order administration timeWriting, pricing and chasing signatures is labor
53Photo documentation and progress reportingRequired by many contracts; someone has to do it

7. Money, risk and timing

#Line itemWhen it bites
54Sales tax on materials — where it applies and who paysVaries by state and by contract type; assume wrong and it's your margin
55Payment processing fees if the client pays by card3% of a large draw is a real line
56Financing / carrying cost of material bought before the drawYou are the bank between purchase and payment
57Price escalation clause on long-lead materialQuotes expire in 30 days; jobs don't
58Long-lead items and the schedule gap they createWindows and cabinets set the critical path
59Contingency for the parts of the scope you truly cannot seeNot a fudge factor — a disclosed line with a defined trigger
60Escalation for work in occupied homes vs. vacantSlower, dirtier, more interruption; measure it once, apply it always
61Winter / weather days on any exterior scopeSchedule risk that becomes a supervision cost
62Bonding or lien waiver processing, where requiredAdministrative and sometimes a real fee
63Overhead recovery — rent, insurance, phone, software, truckMarkup is not profit until overhead is covered

The one habit worth more than the list

Write down your estimated number for each phase. When the job closes, write the actual next to it. Do it for five jobs.

You will find that you are not randomly wrong. You are consistently wrong in the same two or three places — and by roughly the same percentage. That number is more valuable than any template, because it is yours.
