The Subcontractor Scope Checklist

What to nail down before a sub starts — and the eight gaps that become your cost.

Free. No email required.

Why this list exists

A sub's price answers one question: what he will do. Your exposure lives entirely in what he won't, and almost none of that appears in his quote.

Every item below is something that gets done on the job whether or not anyone priced it. If it isn't in his scope, it is in yours.

2. The eight gaps that land on you

These are the ones that show up on nearly every job:

1. Layout and dimensional control. Who sets the lines? If nobody, everyone assumes someone else did, and the tile guy discovers the room is out of square.

2. Cutting and patching for his own work. Does his price include opening the wall he needs to open, and closing it? Usually not.

3. Debris removal. His own waste, to your dumpster, or does it just accumulate in the corner until your labor moves it?

4. Protection of finished work. Adjacent surfaces, floors, and the trade that came before him.

5. Blocking and backing. He needs blocking for his fixtures; the framer already left.

6. Fastening, hangers, straps, and small hardware. Rarely itemized, always needed.

7. Temporary utilities and lighting. He assumes you provide power; you assumed his tools came with it.

8. Punch list return trip. One visit or two? Say it out loud now, because the second visit is where the argument happens.

4. Paper that prevents arguments

  • Current certificate of insurance, naming you as additional insured where required, with the expiry date checked. Not last year's.
  • License, where the trade requires one, and verified rather than described.
  • W-9 or local tax equivalent before the first payment, not after.
  • Lien waivers — conditional on progress payments, unconditional on final. Get them from him and from his suppliers on anything material-heavy.
  • His warranty term, in writing, and whether it matches what you promised the owner. If you gave a two-year warranty on work backed by a 90-day sub warranty, the difference is you.
  • Change order procedure — who can authorize extra work on his end and yours. Most disputed sub extras started with a homeowner talking directly to a sub.

6. The one question worth more than the rest

"What is not included in your price?"

Ask it directly, and wait through the silence. A good sub will list four things immediately, because he has been burned too. A sub who says "everything's included" has either not read the drawings or is planning to have this conversation later, when the work is half done and his leverage is highest.

Write his answer down and attach it to the purchase order. That single paragraph prevents more disputes than any contract clause.

The number worth tracking

Log every sub extra you pay across a year, sorted by which of the gaps above caused it.

It will not be evenly spread. Two categories will account for most of it, and those two are a scope-sheet problem you can fix once — not bad luck you absorb every job.
