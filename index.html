The problem this actually solves

Most estimating products hand you a blank spreadsheet with nicer fonts. The spreadsheet was never the problem. You already know how to add up a job.

The problem is that nobody ever tells you where your estimates break. The job comes in over, you feel it in the bank account, you promise to be more careful next time, and next time you are wrong by the same amount in the same phase — because nothing measured it.

This kit closes that loop. You estimate by phase. At closeout you record what it actually cost. The workbook calculates a correction factor per phase and applies it to your next bid automatically. After five jobs the factors stop moving. That number is yours, it came from your jobs, and no template on the internet has it.

FAQ

What if my jobs are all different sizes?
The factor is a ratio, not a dollar amount, so a $12k bathroom and a $180k whole-house teach it the same lesson. What breaks it is mixing wildly different work types in one file — keep kitchens and additions in separate copies if you do both.
Does it work on Mac?
Yes, Excel for Mac 365 or 2021 and up. Converting to Google Sheets will break some cross-tab formulas.
How long before it is useful?
The checklist is useful on your next bid, today. The factors need about five closed jobs before they stop moving enough to trust — that is honest, and anyone promising faster is guessing.
Who made this, honestly?
Closeout Numbers is a one-person operation, and the English text here is AI-written, working from published construction-cost sources and from a real purchasing-and-job-costing operation — not from a US contractor's personal jobs. Saying otherwise would be an easy way to sound more credible and a bad way to start. The free checklist is the sample: judge the kit by whether the content holds up, not by a byline.
